Pricing the Line Item:
Responsibility Futures as a Supply-Chain of Behavior

Jefferson Richards
Richards Plus, LLC · Item Assure Procurement Pilot
Draft — September 26, 2026 · A synthesis across Survitality of the Synapse (2015), Stockholm Forgiveness of Responsibility (2019), and Put Options on Loyalty (2026)

Abstract

This paper advances a single claim: the stochastic mathematics of inventory and queuing management and the actuarial mathematics of responsibility are the same mathematics, distinguished only by whether the thing being metered is a good or a behavior. Building on the responsibility ratio \(R = I/N\) introduced in Stockholm Forgiveness and formalized in Put Options on Loyalty, we show that the newsvendor critical fractile, the M/M/1 utilization law, and the governed Markov equilibrium each have an exact behavioral reading. We then derive the paper's contribution: a proposal for dynamic line-item indemnification — allocating indemnity capital to individual activities on a project's critical path during the contract term — and we identify the precise regulatory and smart-contract gap that currently blocks it. Drawing on an agent-based morale model, we further show that such indemnifications function as a participation morale floor: they sustain the willingness to transact whose collapse — not any shortage of capital — is what kills a network. We argue this gap, not the mathematics, is the frontier worth working, and that naming it is itself the value-add.

Keywords: responsibility futures, R = I/N, captive insurance, exposure management, newsvendor model, queuing theory, Markov governance, cost of delay, critical path, dynamic indemnification, behavioral economics

1. Motivation: the legibility problem

The topics assembled here — actuarial science, queuing theory, supply-chain logistics, behavioral economics, and moral philosophy — are diverse enough to create a legibility problem for any single reader. The purpose of this paper is to establish a save point: a single, defensible statement of where the ideas converge, written as if for publication, so the diversity becomes an argument rather than a scatter.

The convergence point is the observation, arrived at while studying the newsvendor model, that inventory stock, lead time, delay, cost, and demand are the same variables that govern behavioral economics — and that both are already modeled, in the author's prior work, by the responsibility ratio. What follows makes that identity precise and extracts one actionable proposal from it.

2. The responsibility ratio

Responsibility is determined by how negligent and intentional an entity was, is, and will be. In its original static form the ratio is stated plainly:

$$ R = \frac{I}{N}, \qquad \text{Responsibility} = \frac{\text{Intention}}{\text{Negligence}}. $$

Two operational definitions anchor the terms, borrowed from legal practice: Intention is established by asking “were there duties?” — has the entity held or self-imposed responsibilities — and Negligence by asking “were there damages?” Crucially, \(I\) and \(N\) are not equations but workflows for collecting data in hindsight. A breach that produced no damages does not lower \(R\); it strengthens the risk profile.

The static form divides by zero for a spotless entity. Put Options on Loyalty resolves this in continuous time with an untested baseline \(\lambda\):

$$ R(t) = \frac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t} I(\tau)\,d\tau}{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t} N(\tau)\,d\tau + \lambda}, \qquad \lambda > 0. $$
Reading. A blank slate scores \(0/\lambda = 0\): not infinitely responsible, merely untested. As accumulated negligence grows, \(R\) falls; as accumulated intention grows, \(R\) rises. The ratio is a rate — the behavioral analogue of a fill rate or a service rate.

3. The isomorphism: inventory equations as responsibility equations

We now state the paper's structural result informally as a correspondence. Let a supply chain be a network of queues, a queue a distribution meeting a rate, and the price of that meeting the actuarial identity Premium = Rate × Exposure. Each classical inventory equation then admits a behavioral reading under the map “goods → conduct, stockout → damage, holding cost → over-committed intention.”

3.1 The newsvendor decision is the responsibility decision

The single-period stocking rule sets the order quantity at the critical fractile:

$$ F(Q^{*}) = \frac{C_u}{C_u + C_o}. $$

Here \(C_u\) is the underage (stockout) cost and \(C_o\) the overage cost. Behaviorally, \(C_u\) is the damage of committing too little intention to a duty, and \(C_o\) the negligence of over-committing scarce attention elsewhere. The balancing quantile is precisely the ideal responsibility ratio for the objective — the answer to the correct question, “what \(R\) does this environment require?”, rather than the naive “is this entity responsible?”

3.2 Utilization and the metabolism constraint

The M/M/1 relations give utilization and expected wait:

$$ \rho = \frac{\lambda}{\mu}, \qquad W = \frac{1}{\mu - \lambda}, \qquad \text{stable iff } \lambda < \mu. $$

Stability requires the arrival rate to stay below the service — the metabolic — rate, a framing inherited from Survitality of the Synapse, where the same signal-processing logic governs a synapse, a stomach, and an executive function. As \(\rho \to 1\) the wait diverges: a system run at full utilization seizes. Applied to attention, a population supplied symbols at \(\rho \approx 1\) — total-immersion engagement — is a shop running hot, and the observed “seizing up” of a saturated public sphere is the predicted consequence, not a coincidence.

3.3 Governance as a stationary distribution

Model conduct as a three-state chain — cooperation \(S_1\), untested \(S_2\), negligence \(S_3\) — with transition matrix \(P\). Insurance injects a cost matrix that raises the price of transitioning to \(S_3\), yielding a governed operator \(P_{\text{governed}}\) whose stationary distribution concentrates mass on cooperation:

$$ \boldsymbol{\pi}\,P_{\text{governed}} = \boldsymbol{\pi}, \qquad \pi_{S_1} \to 1. $$
Death table. An actuarial life table is this chain with an absorbing state \(S_\dagger\): once entered it is never left. “Where risk is priced, ceasing to exist” is the boundary condition. The behavioral-economics contribution is to audit each state by its roots and fruits — inputs and outputs of the black box — recovering the maxim that one entity's whole is another's mere attribute.

4. Maintaining a state: duration, immunization, and the change-management floor

If the death table prices the terminal state, Macaulay duration maintains a desired one. Matching the duration of indemnifying cashflows to the liabilities of an attribute immunizes the state against small shocks:

$$ D = \frac{\sum_t t\,C_t (1+y)^{-t}}{\sum_t C_t (1+y)^{-t}}. $$

This licenses a change-management discipline borrowed from product lifecycle management: a proposed feature is admissible only if the prior version's requirements are minimally met (end-to-end and unit tested) and the change increases exposure to inflow exceeding outflow under stated conditions. In the language of the actuarial identity, exposure runs both ways — a Responsibility Future is a written option on an entity's future \(R\), and the seller underwrites the upside as readily as the downside.

5. Contribution: dynamic line-item indemnification

The applied contribution of the Item Assure program follows directly. Captive insurance — capturing one's own risk — already supports incentive structures such as indemnifying a project manager against the failure of a newly accepted parallel project, which converts risk-aversion into a willingness to take on work. Coupled with the Toyota andon-cord practice, where halting the line rewards whoever names an improvement, the mechanism for reducing the coupled cost of delay is traceability on the communications that produce improvements.

Proposal. Build a tool that dynamically allocates indemnifications and capital into a risk pool against individual line items of a critical path, adjustable within the term of the contract. Participants are thereby incentivized to disclose what would affect the schedule, and indemnity capital is placed exactly where exposure concentrates.

The honest obstacle is not mathematical but institutional:

Smart contracts and the prevailing insurance regime do not yet permit line-item indemnifications to be added dynamically within the term of a contract.

Identifying this gap with precision is the paper's central practical value. The mathematics of Sections 2–4 is management, not innovation; it can be trusted to be handled well by any competent operator. The frontier is the contractual instrument that would let a critical path carry priced, dynamic, per-activity indemnity. Item Assure is the attempt to construct it.

There is a second, less obvious payoff of the same instrument, developed formally in Section 6: the indemnifications proposed here are not only risk transfer — they are a morale mechanism. By rewarding the act of speaking up and taking on line-item exposure, the risk pool installs what an agent-based model of transacting populations calls a participation morale floor. The indemnity keeps individuals willing to transact, and it is willingness — not capital — whose collapse kills a network.

6. Morale as the vitality condition of a transacting ecosystem

The responsibility ratio and the indemnification instrument both presuppose a live population of agents willing to act. Section 6 supplies the missing dynamical claim: what keeps that population transacting is morale, and morale is maintained by boundary conditions — including the line-item indemnifications of Section 5. This is the “morale model of conflict” applied to maintained ecosystems of transacting individuals, and it is supported by direct agent-based simulation.

6.1 Morale drives the willingness to transact

Model each agent's morale \(m_i \in [0,1]\) as a function of relative standing in the wealth (or standing) distribution, and let willingness to transact be a Bernoulli draw against it:

$$ m_i = \left(\frac{w_i}{\tilde{w}}\right)^{3/2} \text{ when } w_i < \tilde{w}, \qquad P(\text{transact}_i) = m_i, $$

where \(w_i\) is agent \(i\)'s capital and \(\tilde{w}\) the community median. Below the median, morale decays super-linearly; an agent far below median effectively withdraws. The system-level consequence is stark: a network does not die because its agents are forbidden to participate — it dies because they lose the will to. Dead networks are not poor; they are demoralized. This is the behavioral reading of the M/M/1 seizure in Section 3.2: when standing concentrates, effective participation rate collapses and throughput goes to zero.

6.2 The morale model of conflict

The framing is Lanchester-adjacent: in the classical morale (or breakpoint) model of conflict, a force does not fight to annihilation — it disengages when morale crosses a breakpoint, long before it is physically destroyed. A transacting ecosystem exhibits the same law. The relevant “casualty” is not lost capital but lost willingness; the relevant victory condition is not accumulation but the maintenance of enough morale, across enough agents, to keep the transaction network recurrent. Governance therefore aims not to maximize any single agent's wealth but to keep the population above its collective breakpoint — precisely the stationary-distribution objective of Section 3.3.

6.3 Boundary conditions that maintain morale

An agent-based simulation (100 agents, 300 rounds of morale-weighted bilateral transactions) compares three regimes and yields an unambiguous ordering:

RegimeGiniAvg. moraleTotal transactions
No boundaries (free-market collapse)0.880.5221,908
Soft boundaries (reactive redistribution)0.400.7526,562
Active boundaries (participation incentives)0.380.9233,946

The active regime — roughly 62% more transactions than the free market — runs on three mechanisms that map one-to-one onto the Item Assure instrument:

  1. Participation morale floor — agents who transacted recently cannot fall below a morale threshold; the act of showing up is rewarded, not merely the outcome. This is exactly what a line-item indemnification does: it floors the downside of speaking up, so the coupled-cost-of-delay disclosure of Section 5 becomes a low-morale-risk act.
  2. Structural-advantage dampening — the advantaged still win more often, but compounding is throttled; competition survives, runaway dominance does not. In the responsibility ratio, this caps how fast one agent's accumulated intention crowds the field into negligence-by-withdrawal.
  3. Network-vitality feedback — when aggregate transaction volume is high, every agent receives a small morale bonus; the system rewards its own health. This is the positive-exposure term of Premium = Rate × Exposure made communal.
Conway corollary. A network's output mirrors its communication structure, but communication requires willingness, willingness requires morale, and morale requires believing participation is worth it. Boundary conditions — indemnifications among them — are therefore not constraints on freedom; they are the infrastructure that keeps freedom meaningful and the ecosystem recurrent rather than absorbing.

7. Why the first step dominates

A caution against mistaking managed mathematics for a sound premise. The Theranos episode is instructive precisely because its technical dream may yet be realized within a generation; the failure was diplomacy over virtue — belief and networking outrunning what the technology could then meet. A correct ERP built on a false product premise remains false. As in composition, where perhaps ten poems in two hundred and fifty succeed and their quality is largely fixed at the first line, a venture's responsibility ratio is dominated by intention at the outset, before negligence has time or surface to accumulate. Get the first step right; then let incremental improvement compound.
